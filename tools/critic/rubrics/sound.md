Judge the sound of these clips from a night lake horror game where sound is the player's only danger meter: the frog chorus thins as danger builds and stops when the worst thing comes. Listen with care and quote timestamps. Score each point 0 to 10, then overall.

1. place: does it sound like a small boat on a still lake at night (water on the hull, air, distance)?
2. chorus: is the frog chorus pleasant to live with for ten minutes, and would a player notice it thinning or stopping?
3. silence: when things go quiet, is the quiet itself tense? Is there too much constant noise to ever feel it?
4. cues: is each threat's sound distinct, easy to recognise the second time, and impossible to confuse with a harmless sound?
5. direction: do sounds seem to come from places (shore, under the hull, across the water)?
6. mix: balanced levels, nothing harsh, shrill, clipping or annoying on repeat; loops without an obvious seam.
7. fear_without_gore: is it scary through suggestion (no screams of pain, no gore sounds)?
8. broken_or_ugly: missing, out of sync, cut off, or wrong sounds (10 = nothing).

Top fixes must be concrete: which sound, and what to change (louder, quieter, shorter, lower, drier, a different character, more space before it).
