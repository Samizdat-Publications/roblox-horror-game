## Question

These are the first in-engine screenshots of a Roblox co-op horror fishing game set on a foggy lake at night, built from simple blocky parts. The target look ("Lantern Noir"): near-black teal water, gray-green fog, the boat's warm amber oil lantern as the only strong light, a far warm dock beacon as the way home, crooked tree silhouettes on the shore, a sunken church steeple leaning out of the water. Many players will be on phones in a bright room. The image names say which shot each is: helm (first person from the stern seat), dock (third person looking back at the dock, shack and beacon), steeple (the boat at the steeple far from home), before-black (the first attempt, shown only for comparison).

Score each point 0 to 10, then give an overall score. Be blunt. No praise paragraphs. Do not use em dashes. Under 600 words.

1. readability on a phone
2. the lantern as the one warm pool of light
3. the beacon as the way home
4. mood: lonely and cold rather than merely dark
5. silhouettes (trees, steeple, dock, boat)
6. palette
7. anything broken or ugly

Then list the top five concrete fixes in order of impact, each naming the object, the property to change in plain words (brighter, warmer, larger, more fog, move it), and roughly how much.

## Answer

**Scores**

1. **Readability on a phone: 2/10**
In a bright room, everything except the paper HUD, the flat tan block in the boat, and two tiny light dots will vanish into screen glare. The dock, shore, and upper steeple disappear into black.

2. **Lantern as the one warm pool of light: 3/10**
The lantern bulb is pale lemon yellow instead of warm amber. Its spill on the water reads as muddy olive green, while the bright tan block inside the boat steals focus from the lantern.

3. **Beacon as the way home: 2/10**
In the dock shot, the beacon is a tiny cold white dot casting a chilly pale light, looking like a weak streetlamp rather than a warm refuge. In the steeple shot, it is completely absent or lost.

4. **Mood (lonely and cold rather than merely dark): 3/10**
This is mostly just dark. There is no visible fog volume wrapping around objects, only a thin blue horizon strip sandwiched between pitch black sky and near-black water. Without lit fog, there is no depth or cold atmosphere.

5. **Silhouettes: 3/10**
The shore trees look like blocky signposts and stacked rectangles, not crooked trees. The steeple does not noticeably lean, and its top half vanishes into the black sky. Only the boat and dock read clearly up close.

6. **Palette: 3/10**
You aimed for teal water, gray-green fog, and amber light. You got black voids, a harsh navy horizon stripe, sickly olive ground glow, pale yellow lantern light, and a cold white beacon.

7. **Anything broken or ugly: 3/10** (10 = clean)
The massive flat tan slab at the bottom of the helm shot looks like an untextured placeholder block. The steeple shot has a strange, disconnected greenish smear on the water toward the bottom left. The horizon cuts across the frame like a sharp digital line.

**Overall score: 2.5/10**
It is a clear step up from the pitch-black first attempt, but it fails the phone readability requirement and misses the Lantern Noir target.

---

**Top 5 Concrete Fixes (in order of impact)**

1. **Lake fog and lower sky:** Add much more fog, make it brighter by roughly 2 to 3 times, and shift its color from dark navy to misty gray-green. Raising fog brightness and height lifts silhouettes off the background so phone players can actually see the steeple, trees, and horizon depth.
2. **Boat oil lantern (bulb and light):** Make the color much warmer (deep amber-orange instead of pale yellow), make the light brighter by about 50%, and increase its reach by about 2 times so it casts an unmistakable warm pool across the boat and surrounding water.
3. **Dock beacon (bulb, glow, and light):** Make the color much warmer (amber-orange instead of cold white), make the visible glowing bulb or halo 3 to 4 times larger, and make the light roughly 3 times brighter so it reads as a clear landmark across the fog.
4. **Lake water surface:** Make it slightly brighter (roughly 20% to 30% lighter), shift the color to dark teal instead of muddy black-green, and make it glossier so it catches streaks of amber light from the lantern and beacon.
5. **Boat interior bench/crate and steeple:** Make the flat tan block in the helm shot darker by about 60% and give it wood grain so it stops glowing like a placeholder. Rotate the steeple by 15 to 20 degrees so it visibly leans, and make it roughly 30% taller so it cuts higher into the brightened fog.
