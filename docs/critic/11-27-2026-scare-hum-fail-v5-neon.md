## Question

Watch and listen to this clip from a co-op Roblox horror fishing game (ages 10 and up, often played on phones). It shows the rule called the Hum, played wrong on purpose.

The rule: the whole frog chorus stops at once, then a deep hum rises from under the boat. The crew must stop reeling, cut the outboard motor, put out the lantern and hold still until the frogs sing again. In this clip the player leaves the motor running, so they should fail: the boat is thrown, the catch goes over the side, the player is pulled under (fade to black, a splash or drag sound), and a paper death card should name the rule ("You left the motor running..." style).

Use this rubric. Score each point 0 to 10, then overall. Quote timestamps for everything. Be blunt, no praise paragraphs, no em dashes, under 600 words.

1. cue_clarity: can you hear the frogs stop and the hum rise? Is it unmistakable, and does it arrive before any danger?
2. learnable: after seeing this once, could a 12 year old say what they should have done?
3. time_to_react: enough time between cue and consequence?
4. payoff: is the fail moment readable and startling rather than confusing? Can you SEE anything happen?
5. relief_or_ending: is there a clear end (death card, fade, sound)?
6. clip_worthy: would five seconds of this make a good short video with no explanation?
7. tone: scary but not disturbing, no gore.
8. broken_or_ugly: anything that undercuts it (black screen, missing sound, glitch, timing).

Then list, in order of impact, the top five concrete fixes, each naming the moment and exactly what to change.

## Answer

**Rubric Scores**

1. **cue_clarity: 5/10**
The frogs cut out cleanly at 00:03 after steady croaking from 00:00 to 00:02. However, "Motor: on" pops onto the HUD at 00:05 at the exact second the low hum and water rings start. The hum and motor blur into one muddy engine drone that phone speakers will barely separate, and nothing on deck visually reacts.

2. **learnable: 6/10**
The death card at 00:16 clearly states the rule: motor off, lantern out, hold still when the frogs stop. Before 00:16, though, there is no visible motor, no visible lantern, and no key or touch prompt showing how to turn either off. A 12 year old learns what they failed, not how to do it.

3. **time_to_react: 8/10**
Frogs stop at 00:03, the hum builds from 00:05 to 00:11, and the hit lands at 00:12. Seven to nine seconds is generous for mobile players, though the build goes flat from 00:08 to 00:11.

4. **payoff: 3/10**
Weak and confusing. At 00:12 the boat jerks sideways with a thud, then snaps back upright at 00:13 before fading to dark teal at 00:14 with a splash. You see no threat, no catch goes overboard (Quota stays 0/8), and the camera never feels pulled underwater.

5. **relief_or_ending: 8/10**
The fade at 00:14, black beat at 00:15, and paper card with a low bass hit from 00:16 to 00:19 end the sequence cleanly and legibly.

6. **clip_worthy: 2/10**
Unwatchable as a short clip. From 00:00 to 00:11 it is an almost black static frame, and the 00:12 jolt reads like a physics glitch rather than a monster strike.

7. **tone: 8/10**
Appropriately eerie for ages 10 and up. The sudden silence at 00:03, rising drone from 00:05 to 00:11, and splash fade at 00:14 build dread with zero gore.

8. **broken_or_ugly: 3/10**
The scene from 00:00 to 00:11 is way too dark for phone screens. At 00:12 the water ripple rings tilt with the boat and clip straight into the sky as blocky blue arches. "Motor: on" spawns late at 00:05 and vanishes at 00:12, and the boat awkwardly recovers at 00:13 instead of capsizing.

**Overall: 5/10**

**Top 5 Fixes (Order of Impact)**

1. **Overhaul the fail impact and pull-under (00:12 to 00:14):** Stop the boat from snapping upright at 00:13. Keep it rolling hard from 00:12, launch visible fish or gear over the rail, and plunge the camera beneath the waterline with bubbles and a muffled drag sound before cutting to black at 00:15.
2. **Light the boat and show the lantern and motor (00:00 to 00:12):** Add warm deck illumination from a visible lantern and place a shaking outboard motor on the boat. Players on phones must actually see the boat and the objects they need to shut off.
3. **Separate motor audio from the Hum (00:00 to 00:11):** Have "Motor: on" active with a distinct chugging engine sound from 00:00 instead of starting it at 00:05. Shift the Hum at 00:05 into an unmistakable mid-range resonant drone so phone speakers play both clearly.
4. **Unparent the water ripples from boat rotation (00:05 to 00:12):** Lock the concentric ring effect to the flat water surface so the blue rings do not slice across the sky when the hull tilts at 00:12.
5. **Show Motor and Lantern controls on the HUD (00:00 to 00:11):** Add persistent keybinds and mobile tap buttons for the motor and lantern alongside "Book R Guide G" instead of only showing the cast prompt that disappears at 00:05.

## Action taken

Taken into polish round 3: the ripple rings stay flat on the water instead of tilting into the sky, the boat keeps its roll until the screen is dark, desktop hint buttons for the motor and lantern beside Book and Guide, a little more moonlight with the lantern out.
